# AI Use Reflection

## Tools used and for what

I used Claude (Anthropic) throughout this assessment. For technical support, it created the repository skeleton, explained how to commit through the GitHub web editor and checked that my Markdown rendered correctly. It also supported the substance of the work: it pointed me to key sources, summarised the reports I collected and produced a first draft of the proposal. I then revised and verified that draft section by section in GitHub, as the commit history shows.

## A specific instance of accepting, rejecting or editing AI output

The draft costing table presented a total of $7.0 million with apparent precision, but these figures were Claude's own assumptions rather than sourced data. I labelled them as illustrative assumptions because presenting assumed numbers as fact would mislead the reader, and a policy brief's costings need to be transparent about what is estimated.

## A limitation, challenge or risk

Because Claude's summaries of the reports were fluent and confident, it was easy to accept its framing of a source without questioning it. This happened because the summaries came out already organised around my argument, which could hide findings that did not fit. I read the cited sections of each source myself to make sure the brief reflected what the reports actually said, not just the points that suited my recommendation.
